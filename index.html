Frequently
asked questions

What is the hidden cost of women's health?

It is spend scattered across claims that never adds up to one clear number. Care for one woman spans primary care, behavioral health, imaging, pharmacy, the ER and specialists, often without a women's health label. In one national commercial dataset, women's and maternal health spend totals $216M by specialty and $1.68B by condition. Not all of it is avoidable. The opportunity is to identify where better care can make a measurable difference.

Why doesn't menopause show up as a cost in claims?

Symptoms are billed separately under the specialties that treat them: sleep, anxiety, joint pain and bleeding. The underlying cause often goes uncoded. One report found that only 14.7% of women aged 45 to 64 had evidence of menopause in their medical record.

What is connected women's health care?

One care team, one clinical record, and one plan across a woman's symptoms and life stages, instead of separate referrals to separate specialists, each billing separately.

What results has Sword Women's Health measured?

In a matched claims study of 42,516 commercial members, $2,484 saved per participating member per year, 3.2x gross ROI and 2.2x net, with surgical encounters down 65%. Medical claims only. The methodology was reviewed by Milliman, which did not audit the data, validate savings or ROI, or recalculate results.

How should employers evaluate women's health vendors?

Test against four measures: time to diagnosis, avoidable ER use, specialty coordination and site of care. Then ask five questions about payment, risk and independent review.
